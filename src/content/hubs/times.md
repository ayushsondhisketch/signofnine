## How a clock time is read

A clock time is read as the number it shows. 3:33 carries the reading of 333, 11:11 the reading of 1111, 10:10 the reading of 1010. The digits are added the same way (3 + 3 + 3 = 9) and the core number is found the same way. What the clock adds is context: a time is a "right now" message in the tradition, where a number on a receipt is a general one. A waking time is read as the strongest of all because you were not looking for it.

## The mirror hours

The times people search for most are the mirror hours, 1:11 through 5:55, and the doubles, 11:11 and 12:12. In the UK and Europe the 24-hour clock adds 21:21, 22:22 and 23:23, which American readers rarely see and American sites rarely cover. Each has its own page below, with the reading, the arithmetic and the sleep-cycle or routine explanation where one applies.

## The ordinary explanations, by time of day

Early-morning times (1:11 to 5:55) are mostly waking times, and sleep cycles explain them: ninety-minute cycles from a regular bedtime end at regular points, and people check their phones when they surface. Mid-morning and afternoon times (10:10, 11:11, 12:12, 1:23) are routine times: a break, a school pick-up, the end of a meeting. Evening times (21:21, 22:22, 23:23) coincide with the heaviest phone use of the day. None of this stops a time from being a useful prompt. It does mean the sighting itself is not the evidence.
